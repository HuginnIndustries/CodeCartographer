# E09 dogfood: lifting a real self-audit spec into slices

**Date:** 2026-09-22. **Source artifact:** `self-audit/2026-09-15-v0.25.0-full-with-deep-audit/findings/reimplementation-spec/reimplementation-spec.md` (written against the pre-E09 template). **Method:** a bounded manual conversion of two modules (document codec, pipeline engine) into the E09 shape, lifted with `liftSlices`, handed to `buildChangePlan`, then each slice inspected by hand for whether its scenarios actually prove its promise. Excerpt and script under `docs/engineering/e09-dogfood/`.

## What happened

- The unconverted artifact is refused, and the refusal names every missing structure at once: `missing-section@Slices`, plus `missing-column@Acceptance Scenarios` for both `Scenario ID` and `Tier`. An earlier draft of the lifter returned on the first missing section and hid the second; the pre-E09 artifacts are what exposed that. This is pinned as a test.
- The converted excerpt (15 scenarios, 8 slices) lifts with zero errors and plans clean in E04. Every scenario id the templates teach (`S-01`, `SL-01`) is a valid E01 local id.
- Converting took roughly twenty minutes by hand for two modules. The whole spec has 41 scenarios across ~10 modules; a full conversion is an afternoon, not a script, because deciding which scenario proves which promise is the actual planning work.

## Does each scenario prove its slice's promise?

This is the inspection the issue asked for. Honest verdicts, not a checklist.

| Slice | Promise | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| SL-01 | reader refuses what the source refuses, with named errors | **Partial** | S-01/S-06/S-07 each prove ONE refusal with ONE named error. They do not prove "what the source refuses" in general; nothing here enumerates the source's refusal set. A reviewer would accept SL-01 on these and still not know whether e.g. anchors or merge keys are refused. The promise is wider than the proof. |
| SL-02 | prototype-safe construction on every mapping and sequence-merge path | **Partial** | S-02 and S-03 prove the two paths the original audit found. "Every path" is a universal claim; two scenarios prove two instances. The slice should either narrow its promise to the two paths or a scenario should enumerate the constructors. |
| SL-03 | writer round-trips byte-for-byte, preserving order and scalar type | **Yes** | S-04 (scalar type), S-08 (order), S-09 (determinism) together cover the three named properties. This is the one slice whose promise and proof match in scope. |
| SL-04 | documented flow-collection policy | **Yes, but weak** | S-05 proves a policy exists and is followed. "Policy-documented outcome" is a scenario that accepts either behaviour; it proves consistency with a document, not a behaviour. | SL-05 | cursor reports eligible / stuck / complete | **Yes** | S-10 is a three-case scenario that names all three outcomes. |

**Net:** 5 of 8 slices have proof that matches their promise. 2 (SL-01, SL-02) make universal promises with instance proofs. 1 (SL-04) is deliberately weak and correctly tiered.

## What this shows about the E09 shape

1. **The structure catches nothing about proof *strength*, only proof *existence*.** SL-01 lifts and plans clean. The lifter and E04 both agree it is a valid plan. It is not a good one. That judgment is a reviewer's, as the lifter's header says, and this dogfood is the evidence that the reviewer step is load-bearing rather than ceremonial. The gap is by design; the note is so nobody mistakes "lifts clean" for "proves its promise".
2. **The empty-proof-list rule was not exercised by this conversion.** Every slice in the excerpt was written with its proofs in the same pass, so the refusal never fired here. It is exercised by the tests (`empty-proof-list` on the lifter side, and the E04 agreement test that hands the same refused slice to `buildChangePlan`), not by this dogfood. Saying so is more useful than a made-up anecdote.
3. **Tiering forced a decision the old table let me skip.** Giving S-05 and S-14 a tier meant deciding whether flow-collection policy was minimum-viable. The old spec's prose tiers said "document codec" was minimum-viable as a whole; per-scenario tiering exposed that one of its scenarios is not.
4. **Old artifacts are not silently reinterpreted.** They are refused with a complete list. A session converting one gets all the reasons in one pass.

## What review found after this note was first written

The adversarial review (`deleg_1ebf14c6`) broke both headline claims of the first lifter with live reproductions, and this note would be dishonest without them:

- **The reader dropped rows the author wrote, silently.** A GFM-legal row without a leading pipe, or a blank line an author used to group rows, ended the table and every row after it vanished with `errors: []`. That is the exact outcome the commit claimed to prevent; the `missing-id` branch only ever saw rows that survived the reader. The reader now follows GFM's rules (optional leading pipe, `\|` literal, blank lines do not end a table, prose between rows is refused as `interrupted-table`, ragged rows refused by cell count, fenced code invisible, a duplicated heading refused rather than picked).
- **"Refuses on exactly the grounds E04 refuses" was false three ways.** A dependency cycle lifted clean (E04's own comment says E01's self/unknown checks cannot catch `a -> b -> a`; I had reproduced exactly those two and omitted the one E04 added). An empty deliverable and an empty scenario description lifted clean and E04 refused both. All three were invisible to my agreement tests because the `planFromLift` helper *repaired* them — `s.deliverable || s.id`, `description || "(from artifact)"`, `depends_on: []` — before E04 saw them. The helper now carries lifted values verbatim. A test helper that fixes the input is a test that cannot fail.
- **The "equivalent mutant" claim was wrong.** `| - |  | - |` is a data row to the tight regex and a separator to the loose one — an observable difference, not the bare-colons case I had checked. The separator regex is gone entirely; the delimiter row is now *the line after the header* and nothing else, which is what GFM says.
- **A misspelled column hid every row-level defect.** The column gate was all-or-nothing; now only the id column gates the row loop.
- The shipped templates' blank example rows were themselves refused by E04 (empty description). They now carry placeholder text in parentheses that a session overwrites.

The three findings recorded as sound — no prototype pollution, no ReDoS at 25KB, language-agnostic spec carries no proof commands — held.

## Limits

- Two modules of ten. The other eight are unconverted; their scenarios (locks, refresh, library, sub-agents) are more cross-cutting and the slice boundaries are less obvious. The 5-of-8 ratio should not be extrapolated.
- No phase was actually *run* against a disposable workspace with the new template; the conversion was by hand. Running the `reimplementation-spec` phase end to end with a session filling the new Slices table is the missing half of step 5 and is recorded as such. It needs a model session and a disposable workspace, not a test.
- `depends_on` was lifted but not carried into E04 (`depends_on: []` in the script), because record dependencies bind by `RecordId`, which is assigned at store time. Mapping `SL-01` → `slc_…` is a store-time step that is not part of E09.
