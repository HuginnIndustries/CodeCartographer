A few candid observations from this session:

The phase pipeline (architecture → defect-scan → contracts → protocols → porting → reimplementation-spec) earned its keep. Each phase produces a self-contained deliverable, downstream phases cite upstream by section anchor, and `status.yaml` + `THREAD_LOG.md` together form a chain of custody that made it possible to do this revision pass without re-reading the source repo. The citation convention (`[contracts §X]`, `[protocols §X]`, `[porting Finding N]`) is the load-bearing piece — when I needed to assess whether a delta was widening scope or correcting a previously-pinned contract, I could trace back to the originating phase. Without those citations the triage would have been guesswork.

The "spike session as a separate concept, not a phase" pattern is good. It produces evidence artifacts (per-spike report + `CONSOLIDATED-DELTA.md`) that feed back into the spec through pre-implementation revisions without reopening any phase, and `current_phase` stays `complete`. That maps well to how this kind of work actually happens — you finish the spec, then real-world friction surfaces gaps, and you fix them in place rather than pretending the spec was wrong from the start.

What I'd actually change: there's no template or `SKILL.md` for the spec-delta-application step itself. This session ran on the discipline embedded in your prompt — apply spec-blockers, triage clarifications, defer nice-to-haves to a backlog, preserve traceability, re-run validation without softening criteria, write a disposition table, append to `THREAD_LOG`, leave `status.yaml` untouched. That's a reusable workflow. I invented the `DELTAS-APPLIED.md` table format in this session; it should be standardized. And the new `[revised per spike-N-<slug>.md §Δn]` citation form I added to the front-matter evidence-convention list should be canonical, not per-session.

The "don't apply deltas verbatim without thinking" instruction was the one that actually paid off in this session. Δ-15 ("update all 11 nested-tool exports to `Promise<...>`") was wrong on close reading — three of the eleven are local-only with no IPC, and `ALL_TOOLS` isn't a function. A rubber-stamp would have produced an incorrect spec. The framework's templates would benefit from codifying that posture explicitly: spike reports recommend, the spec-delta session decides, and pushback is part of the workflow rather than an exception.

Two minor things. `THREAD_LOG.md` already has a duplicated spike-session entry (the `## 2026-05-02 (spike session)` block appears twice, identical, lines 136-158 and 159-181). Probably an earlier session double-appended; the framework has no de-dup guard, so entries can quietly drift toward redundancy. And the validation criteria are outcome-shaped ("required behaviors are stated") rather than process-shaped, which is correct for the goal but means the "fix the spec body, don't soften the criterion" rule in your prompt was doing real work that isn't currently encoded anywhere a future session would find it.

Net: framework is sound and the discipline holds up under revision pressure. The two gaps worth closing are a `spec-delta-application` skill/template, and a small linting pass over `THREAD_LOG.md` to catch dup entries. Everything else worked as advertised.
